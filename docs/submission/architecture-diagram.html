About this project

Built for a hackathon track about safe, coordinated agents.

The All Things Agentic Hackathon's Fortified Enterprise Fleet track asks for multiple specialized agents working together under real governance — permission checks, security scanning, and human approval — instead of one chatbot with tools bolted on.

LifeGrid applies that to something ordinary: planning real household tasks, like a family trip. The rest of this deck walks through how it's actually built, not just what it does.

The problem

Real life takes too many apps to plan.

A simple family trip means one tab for flights, another for hotels, a group chat about the budget, a calendar you have to check by hand — and then hoping nobody forgets something important.

🧭
Too scattered
Research, budget, and schedule all live in different places, so nothing talks to each other.
💸
Too risky to automate
Handing an AI your calendar and your card is scary if it can act without asking first.
🔁
Too repetitive
You re-explain your preferences — dietary needs, budget limits — every single time.

Meet LifeGrid

One request. A whole team handles it.

You type one plain-English goal and a budget. LifeGrid splits the work across a small team of specialist AI agents — each with one job — and only acts once you've said yes.

Plans it for you
Research, scheduling, and budgeting happen automatically from one request.
Remembers your preferences
Things you've told it before — allergies, budget limits — persist across requests.
Never spends without asking
Anything over $100, or any booking, pauses for your yes/no first.
Stops itself if something looks wrong
A detected threat halts the whole run — covered on the next few slides.

The pattern

Two Google ADK building blocks, nested.

ADK gives you a few ways to compose agents. LifeGrid uses two of them together — not a custom framework, just these two, nested.

SequentialAgent
Runs its child agents one after another, in a fixed order set in code. There's no branch where a step gets skipped — the order isn't a decision anything makes at runtime.
ParallelAgent
Runs its child agents at the same time and waits for all of them to finish. Used only where the agents don't depend on each other's output.

LifeGrid's whole pipeline is one SequentialAgent — security check, then research, then budget, then write-up — with a ParallelAgent nested inside it for the four research agents. Next slide shows exactly how that's laid out.

Built-in safety

Three checks, always on — not optional add-ons.

Trust isn't a feature you bolt on later. These run on every single request, with no way to turn them off.

🛡️
Model Armor
Scans every request for hidden or malicious instructions — including ones disguised inside a link. If it finds one, the whole run stops right there.
✋
Spend approval
One rule, always enforced: anything over $100, or any booking at all, pauses and waits for your yes.
🔒
Zero-trust access
Every agent has a fixed, narrow list of what it's allowed to touch. Anything outside it is blocked automatically.
